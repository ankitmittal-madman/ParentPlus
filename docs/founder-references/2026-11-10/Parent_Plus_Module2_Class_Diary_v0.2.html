    The problem, from first principles

    The coach's attention is the scarcest thing in a class of 3 to 7 year olds. Every feature here is judged by one test: does it take that attention away from the children?

    What happens today

    Attendance is in a notebook or nowhere. Updates are a hurried WhatsApp photo. Fees are a mental list and an awkward message. Parents ask the same questions every week.

    Why it happens

    All of this admin competes with teaching, and it is done by typing on a phone, alone, between batches or after a long day. Tools built for schools assume an office and staff.

    What changes with the diary

    The app already knows the schedule, the roster and today's plan, so it drafts everything. The coach speaks, picks or approves. Parents get a calm, professional stream of updates, reminders and memories.

    What reaches parents: the UI-agnostic contract

    Your prototype's side panel mapped coach actions to parent-app surfaces. v0.2 turns that map into a contract: each coach action creates a typed item with fixed fields and rules. Any interface (today's PWA, a native app, WhatsApp, even a voice assistant) decides how to show it. The prototype surface is listed only as one example rendering.

    How this module grows out of the pilot without breaking it, connects to Module 1, and stays swappable while running on free services.

    The data model: extend the pilot, never rename

    Teal boxes are your MVP's tables with new columns added. The bold box, class_sessions, is the new anchor that everything else hangs off.

    How Module 1 plugs in. classes.curriculum_version_id points a batch at a Module 1 course. Each class_session then knows its session plan (curriculum_node), so updates, materials reminders and observation chips (skills and milestones) come pre-filled.
